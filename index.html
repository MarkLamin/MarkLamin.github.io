About

I am a first-year Biostatistics PhD student at McGill University supervised by Celia Greenwood, currently focusing on statistical methods for DNA methylation. Previously, I received my MS in Biostatistics from the University of Minnesota's School of Public Health, Division of Biostatistics and Health Data Science. Additional application areas of interest include population genetics and tumor imaging data while additional methodological areas include unsupervised learning, non-linear dimensionality reduction, bayesian modeling, and spatial statistics. I am interested in pursuing academic research.

Some of my previous research involves ancestry assignment for admixed and diverse populations. This research effort is part of the Genomic Data Commons Project and is advised by Dr. Saonli Basu. Before this, I conducted research under Dr. Sandra Safo and Dr. Thierry Chekouo on multi-omics data integration to find biomarkers that characterize the Alzheimer's Disease progression. My interest in omics began in 2022, as a fellow in the Colorado Summer Institute in Biostatistics, where I worked on implementing spectral clustering methods for metabolomics and proteomics data in the COMBAT clinical trial.

I have a strong technical expertise, with proficiency in languages such as R, Python, and SQL. I am also experienced in using the linux shell for high-performance computing, specifically using plink and submitting batch jobs for my population genetics research. Additionally, I have experience in developing interactive web apps with R Shiny, recently doing so as a Senior Data Scientist Intern at Tactile Medical in Summer 2024, where I used my technical skills to support data-driven decision-making for marketing teams and stakeholders.

I am also passionate about education, having taught and tutored math, and served as a Technical Trainer at Epic Systems Corporation where I taught courses in analytics software, relational databases, and clinical reporting. I am very interested in statistics education, having taken courses and attended meetings in the Twin Cities StatEd community. In Fall 2024, I was a Teaching Assistant for PubH 7405 (Biostatistical Inference 1), leading lab sessions in statistics, linear algebra, and R programming for first-year biostatistics MS and PhD students. In Spring 2025, I was a Teaching Assistant for PubH 6450 (Biostatistics 1), a first-semester course in biostatistics for MPH students.
